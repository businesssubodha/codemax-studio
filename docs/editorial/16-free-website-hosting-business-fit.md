# Free Website Hosting: What Does Your Business Still Need to Pay For?

Planned publication: 2026-10-16

Primary keyword: free website hosting

Supporting keywords: free website, business website

SEO title: Free Website Hosting for Business: What to Check | CodeMax

Meta description: Considering free website hosting? Check domain costs, forms, editing, usage limits and support before choosing a setup for your small business.

Free website hosting can be a sensible choice for a small business with a straightforward site. The important question is whether the complete setup supports your customers and the person maintaining it. A hosting bill of zero does not automatically mean a website costs nothing to operate. Separate the services before comparing proposals.

## Start with the kind of website you need
A brochure website presents information such as services, opening hours, photographs and contact details. A booking system or online shop also has to handle changing records and transactions. Write down what happens when a customer clicks the main button: do they call, submit an enquiry, reserve a time or purchase a product? That answer helps define which services are required.

A static website can be a good fit when most content is prepared before visitors arrive. However, its contact form still needs a way to process submissions and send messages. A site that loads successfully while its enquiry service has stopped working is not meeting the business requirement.

## Separate hosting from the other bills
Use five lines in your budget: domain registration, website hosting, business email, connected services and ongoing work. Put the supplier, renewal date and responsible person beside each line. If a supplier bundles these together, ask for the inclusions and what happens when you cancel.

Domain registration gives you control of an address while the registration remains current. Hosting serves the website. A mailbox lets your team receive and send email. These are separate jobs even when they appear on one invoice. You should not assume a free hosting plan includes your own domain name or a business inbox.

## Check limits against real usage
For a concrete example, Cloudflare documents a 20,000-file limit for Pages sites on its Free plan at the time of research. Its documentation also lists other limits. Connected services can have their own allowances, so a static site's capacity does not establish the capacity of its email or form processing.

Ask the person building your site to identify the relevant limits and explain the response if one is reached. Will requests stop, will someone receive an alert, or can charges start under the chosen configuration? Read the current plan terms before deciding. Avoid treating any provider's free allowance as a permanent business guarantee.

## Include the cost of making changes
Who will update a service description or publish a new article? A technically efficient website may still be inconvenient if every small edit requires skills your team does not have. Ask for a demonstration using a change you expect to make regularly, rather than a generic tour of the software.

Document how the site is backed up, how an earlier version is restored and who can deploy an update. If the developer is away, another authorised person should be able to locate the accounts and instructions. Free infrastructure is more useful when the business can operate it confidently.

## Make the decision with a simple checklist
Before launch, confirm that the domain is in the right account, HTTPS works, the enquiry reaches the intended inbox and the site can be restored. Record the first-year costs and renewal costs, including optional support. Keep a copy of the scope alongside the account details.

For a Melbourne small business, the practical goal is a dependable customer journey with costs you understand. Choose free hosting when it fits that goal. Choose another arrangement when its editing, support or application requirements justify it; the monthly hosting price alone cannot make that decision.
