# Google Business Profile and Your Website: Fix These 6 Mismatches

Scheduled: 2026-10-04 from 9 am Melbourne time.

Primary keyword: Google Business Profile website consistency

Related terms: local business website details, Google Maps website information

SEO title: Google Business Profile and Website: 6 Checks | CodeMax

Meta description: Check six common mismatches between your Google Business Profile and website, from hours and services to booking links and service areas.

A customer finds your business on Google, clicks through and sees a different phone number or an old opening-hours message. Even when both pages load correctly, the experience raises a question: which information should they trust? This short audit helps a service business make its public information consistent and easier to act on.

## Check identity, contact details and hours

Compare the business name shown on your profile, website header and contact page. Use the name your business actually uses rather than adding extra search terms to look more relevant. Then test the telephone links and email address. A number written correctly in visible text can still have the wrong destination behind the button.

Review ordinary hours and any upcoming closures. If the office is closed but calls are handled by another team, explain that arrangement accurately. Keep a note of who updates each system so a change does not depend on someone remembering every account.

## Compare services and service areas

Read your profile’s services beside your website’s service pages. Remove offers you no longer provide, and explain significant exclusions where they help a customer choose. A broad label such as “maintenance” should lead to a page describing what maintenance means in your business.

Check locations just as carefully. Serving customers in an area does not mean having an office there. Make the distinction clear on your website. Google describes local ranking in terms of relevance, distance and prominence; matching your wording does not erase the distance between a searcher and your business.

## Test the website and booking destinations

Open the profile’s website link as a visitor. Does it lead to the current site, and is the next step obvious? If you use a booking link, confirm that it opens the correct service and still accepts bookings. A link to an expired campaign page can create an avoidable dead end.

Use a small audit table: item, profile value, website value, correction and owner. The six rows are business identity, contact details, hours, services, areas and destination links. That is enough structure to keep the check repeatable without turning it into a large reporting exercise.

## Add evidence after the basics agree

Once the information matches, improve the page visitors land on. Include genuine work photographs, a clear service explanation and approved customer feedback where available. Avoid invented reviews, exaggerated availability or location claims. The aim is to help a nearby customer decide whether your business fits their need.

Repeat the audit when your services, team or hours change. A quarterly reminder may be sufficient for a stable business, while seasonal businesses need more frequent attention. Consistency is maintenance work: small updates made reliably are more useful than a one-off profile overhaul that becomes outdated.
