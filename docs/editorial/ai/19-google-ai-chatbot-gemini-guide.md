If you search for Google AI chatbot or AI Google, you are probably looking for Gemini, Google's AI assistant. However, Google uses AI across several products, so a search feature, a developer platform and the Gemini app are not interchangeable. Begin by identifying which experience you want to use.

## Start with the Gemini app
Google presents Gemini as an assistant for tasks such as writing, planning and brainstorming. Its official website is the sensible starting point for finding the app and checking availability. Features can depend on the account, location, device and subscription, so a demonstration in a video may not match your own screen.

For a first experiment, choose a low-stakes task you already understand. Ask for three alternative introductions to a piece of writing, or an explanation of a concept you can verify. Avoid judging the entire product from one entertaining answer or assuming a confident response establishes accuracy.

## Give it something specific to work with
Compare these requests: help me plan something, and create a two-hour indoor activity plan for four adults using these materials. The second request supplies a task, constraints and a result you can evaluate. Add the output format if it matters, such as a checklist or a short table.

A useful follow-up is to ask which parts of the answer depend on assumptions. Read those assumptions yourself. If the answer includes a current opening time, price or factual claim, check the original source before acting on it. Asking the assistant to check itself is not equivalent to independent verification.

## Understand connected apps before enabling them
Google describes ways Gemini can work with connected Google apps. That can reduce the effort of moving information between services, but the available connections and permissions should be reviewed in your account. Check which information a connection can read and what actions it can perform.

Use a harmless example first, such as finding a non-sensitive document you created for the test. Confirm that the result refers to the correct item. A summary of the wrong document can sound convincing while being completely unhelpful.

## Separate answers from completed actions
A response describing an email or a calendar entry does not by itself prove that anything was sent or saved. When using action-capable features, look for the application's confirmation and inspect the destination. This is particularly useful when multiple accounts or similarly named files are involved.

Decide in advance which actions you want to approve. For example, drafting a message can be automatic while sending it remains a deliberate step. The right boundary depends on the task and the permissions the current product provides.

## Keep a small comparison record
Save the task, the date, the account features used and what you had to correct. Revisit the same task if you later compare another assistant. That gives you a practical view of usefulness without relying on a universal best-chatbot claim. For changing features, follow Google's current product documentation rather than an old screenshot.
